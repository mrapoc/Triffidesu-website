# Funding case: Triffid Explorer Scouts, Ludlow

Source text for grant applications, letters to potential funders, and press
enquiries. Keep this file as the single version and edit it here rather than in
whatever copy has been pasted into a form — the website copy on
`support.html` and `impact.html` is written from the same facts, so if a number
changes it needs changing in all three.

Deliberately contains no membership counts. The roll changes every September
and any figure written down here is wrong by half term. Six two-person tents is
a fact about equipment we own, so that one stays.

---

## In one paragraph

Triffid Explorer Scout Unit is a volunteer-run Scout unit in Ludlow, South
Shropshire, for young people aged 14 to 18. We nearly did not reopen after
Covid. We have rebuilt to the point where there are more Explorers on the roll
than the unit can sleep, and this year's summer camp filled every tent we own
and filled the minibus without the whole unit coming. We are asking for help
with tents and transport so that the ceiling on how many young people we can
take is not set by equipment.

## Who we are

- Explorer Scout Unit for 14 to 18 year olds, part of Shropshire Severn and
  Teme District and of The Scout Association.
- We meet at Ludlow Scout Hut, Station Drive, Ludlow SY8 2PQ, Mondays 7–9pm in
  term time.
- Two Explorer Scout Leaders, both DBS-checked and safeguarding-trained,
  between them nearly sixty years in Scouting.
- Entirely volunteer-run. No salaries, no office, no management charge, no
  fundraising percentage.
- Members travel in from across South Shropshire and the Welsh and
  Herefordshire borders: Craven Arms, Bishop's Castle, Clun, Bucknell,
  Leintwardine, Wigmore, Knighton, Presteigne and Cleobury Mortimer.

## The problem we are asking you to solve

Covid did something to Explorer Scouts that it did not do to the younger
sections. The section runs from 14 to 18, so a couple of years of not meeting
does not dent the numbers, it removes them: the oldest age out on schedule and
nobody comes up behind them. We lost cohort after cohort that way, and whether
the unit reopened at all was a genuine question.

It reopened, and has been rebuilt one Monday at a time since, with more girls
joining than at any point before. The unit is now larger than its equipment.

- **Tents.** We own six two-person tents. That is twelve sleeping spaces on
  paper and fewer in practice, because boys and girls sleep separately and a
  two-person tent then does not always take two. This summer's camp used every
  one of them.
- **Transport.** The same camp filled the minibus. Where we can go is now
  partly decided by how many seats we can lay hands on, which caps the size of
  the unit for reasons unconnected to how many young people want to be in it.

## What we are asking for

| | |
|---|---|
| **Three more patrol tents** | Enough to sleep everyone currently on the roll, with slack for however the boy/girl split falls and for those joining in September. |
| **Transport** | Either the hire cost of a second vehicle on larger trips, or support towards a vehicle of our own. |

Either can be met in full or in part. Equipment donated in good working order is
as useful to us as cash — ask us first and we will tell you what is on the list.

Beyond the immediate need, transport is what would let us take these young
people to cultural and heritage sites around the UK, alongside the activities
they come for. For a teenager in rural South Shropshire, that is not otherwise
on offer.

## Why funding a unit like this goes further

There is nothing between your money and the young people. Weekly subs of £15 a
month cover the ordinary running of a Monday night; everything beyond that comes
from grants, donations and families. Equipment is bought once, owned by the unit,
and used by every Explorer who comes afterwards.

Even this website costs the unit nothing. A local firm, Fresh Tech, built it
free of charge and continues to host, maintain and add to it for nothing.

We also run the Duke of Edinburgh's Award as a licensed centre, at £33.50 for
Bronze against the £150 to £600 most schools charge for the same award, which
puts it within reach of families for whom the school route is not.

Nobody here has ever missed a trip because of what their family could pay.

## Our track record with funders

**Tesco, 2019 — camping equipment.** Kitted out our camping store from nothing:
patrol tents, an event shelter, a gas cooker, and the kitchen utensils and
cutlery we still cook and eat with. Seven years on, all of it is still in
service. Every Explorer who has camped with us since has slept under something
that grant bought. The tents in it are the six we have now outgrown.

**Lodge of the Marches, 2026 — £400 towards summer camp.** Put into the cost of
getting to Pembrokeshire and into holding the price down for every family who
wanted to come. *"This is a cause we are happy to support."*

Every supporter is named publicly on our Wall of Honour at
https://www.triffidesu.co.uk/support.html alongside the specific thing their
money bought.

## What we can give a funder in return

- A named thank you on the website, against the specific thing funded.
- Photographs of the camp or the equipment in use.
- A short written report for a committee or trustees, in whatever format is
  required.
- A leader who will come and talk to your group about what happened to the
  money.
