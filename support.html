Why It Matters

Explorer Scouts is meant to be for everyone. In practice, the trips young people remember for the rest of their lives — a week away, a proper expedition, a big day out — are the ones that cost money.

Donations and grants let us do three things: keep what families pay as low as we can, buy kit the unit could never fund out of weekly subs, and quietly help out where a young person would otherwise miss the trip. Nobody is ever told they can't come because of money.

It also means the equipment belongs to the unit rather than a hire company. Kit is bought once and then used by every Explorer who comes after — including ones who were still at primary school when it arrived.

The Shopping List

Two things we're saving for. Neither is really about the kit — both buy back time and freedom on camp.

A week's food, anywhere we choose to pitch

At the moment, where we camp is partly decided by how far it is to a shop. Ice blocks are melted by the evening and fresh food doesn't last beyond the first day, so someone drives out for more — every day, on every camp, taking a chunk of the programme with them.

A 12V compressor fridge/freezer running off a solar panel and portable power unit ends the daily run. A full week's food stays properly cold on any site, with no mains hook-up and no shop needed within reach.

Fund this one and you're buying back hours of every camp, and the freedom to pitch somewhere for the walking rather than the shopping.

GPS trackers, so they can go on ahead

The whole point of a DofE expedition is that young people navigate it themselves, with no adult walking alongside. That independence is what makes the achievement real. It is also the part that keeps leaders awake.

A set of GPS trackers lets us give them the genuine article. They walk their own route and make their own decisions, and we know they're safe without ever appearing over the hill to check.

Fund these and you're not really buying kit. You're buying the moment a sixteen-year-old works out that nobody is coming to help — and that they've got it anyway.

Either can be met in full, in part, or by donating the equipment itself. If you'd like to know what something costs before you decide, just ask — we'll send you the actual figures.

Gift Aid makes your donation worth 25% more

We can claim Gift Aid, so if you're a UK taxpayer every £100 you give becomes £125 — at no extra cost to you. All it takes is a short declaration with your name and home address, confirming you've paid enough UK Income or Capital Gains Tax to cover the amount we reclaim.

If you pay tax at the higher or additional rate, you can also claim the difference back yourself through Self Assessment. We'll send you the declaration form when you get in touch — it takes about a minute.

Ways to Help

All of these genuinely help, and none of them have to be large.

A one-off donation

Any amount. Small donations pool together into camp places and kit just as well as large ones do.

Equipment

Camping gear in good working order is as useful to us as cash — and often more so. Ask us what's on the list before you buy anything.

Nominate us for a grant

Plenty of employers, supermarkets and community funds let staff or customers put forward a local cause. That is exactly how the 2019 Tesco grant reached us.

Clubs, lodges and businesses

If your organisation supports local youth causes, we'll happily come and talk about what we do — and give you a proper thank you here afterwards.
